# RANA — A Novel of Warsaw

**Genre:** Adult literary historical fiction
**Setting:** Berlin, 1928–1932 · Warsaw under occupation, 1939–1945
**Estimated length:** 20 chapters · ~80,000 words · ~4,000 words per chapter
**Authorial mode:** In the epic, cross-cultural sweep of James Clavell — honor, enmity, obligation, and the collision of two proud, ruined nations, seen through intimate eyes.

---

## Note on the telling

This is a mature novel. It engages occupation, deportation, executions, medical triage, and the moral emergency of war without flinching — but it is not misery-tourism, and it is not titillation. Intimacy between characters is written with adult intensity and emotional truth; the most private moments are kept on the page's dignified side of the curtain. The violence is purposeful and seen through a surgeon's eyes. Period dialogue is raw and unfiltered — prejudice is depicted, never endorsed.

---

## The novel in one paragraph

Warsaw, November 1943. Poland's only female surgeon in a leading position is called to the operating theatre as a Wehrmacht convoy thunders into the hospital courtyard with a critically wounded staff officer. Under the field dressings she finds the face she has not seen since a Berlin gymnasium of the late 1920s, where the two of them were legends: Sylwia Kotarska, the Polish girl who would not bow to anyone, and Ludwig Schoeler, the Prussian artillery brat she hated with an intimacy greater than friendship. The Wehrmacht needs its man kept alive. The Polish Home Army wants him dead. Her oath — the one thing she has left that the occupier cannot confiscate — says save him. Saving him may be the most dangerous thing she has ever done.
