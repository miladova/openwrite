# CHAPTER THIRTEEN

## The Canary Trap

**Warsaw — Mid-December 1943**

Under ordinary room light, anthracene-sulfate compounded with finely milled quinine bisulfate looked no different from dry radiator dust on painted iron.

At eleven o'clock on the morning of December eleventh, inside the pitch-black radiological fluoroscopy cabinet off Pavilion Three's ground-floor corridor, Sylwia flipped the brass toggle switch of the Hanau quartz mercury-vapor Wood's lamp. Normally the deep violet-black beam served to screen ragged children from Wola orphanages for *Microsporum* scalp ringworm, which fluoresced bright apple-green in the dark.

"Hold the lower corner of the barium plate steady for me a second, Roman, while I center the lead diaphragm," Sylwia asked casually over her shoulder as Dr. Roman Karski stepped behind the black oilcloth curtain carrying Ward Four's morning chest films.

"Make it quick, Pani Doktor, admissions is backed up to the stairs," Karski muttered irritably. Since Tuesday his skin had taken on a waxy, greenish-tallow sheen; twice during yesterday's appendectomy his hand had jerked badly enough on the retractor to bruise rectus muscle, and he kept wiping cold sweat off his upper lip with a crumpled handkerchief. Reaching across the lead-glass frame in the pitch dark to steady the cassette corner for her, he rested his bare right thumb and index finger squarely inside the invisible ultraviolet cone of the Wood's filter.

Sylwia did not move a muscle in the darkness.

Glowing stark, unmistakable, ghostly ice-blue along the whorls of Roman Karski's right thumbpad, index fingertip, and the inner crease of his palm—shaped precisely like the brass receiver hook of the courtyard public payphone and the corner clip of Tuesday's salted Wehrmacht rail-disinfection carbon in the pharmacy safe—shone the quinine-anthracene trace Hauptmann Brandt had mixed for her forty-eight hours ago.

*Roman.*

Even expecting it since last week, seeing that electric blue glow on the hand of a Polish physician who had shared bread crusts and carried wounded children down burning stairs beside her in September thirty-nine turned Sylwia's stomach briefly to lead.

"Thank you, Roman," she said evenly, snapping off the violet Wood's lamp before his pinprick eyes could look down and wonder why his own fingertips shone like corpse-candles. Flipping on the red darkroom bulb instead, she took the cassette calmly from his hands. "Go take twenty minutes for hot tea in the doctors' room; you look chilled through."

Karski didn't want tea, and both of them knew it. Thirty minutes earlier, huddled behind a frozen locomotive water-tower near Dworzec Gdański while December sleet blew sideways off the Vistula, Karski had endured the worst ten minutes of his thirty-five years across an upturned crate from SS-Sturmbannführer Otto Reinhardt.

Reinhardt had not offered Dutch cocoa or polite Bavarian pear drops today.

*"Polizei-Regiment Twenty-Two encircled three empty charcoal camps in Kampinos Forest on Wednesday morning, Doktor Karski,"* Reinhardt had said softly, cold wind whistling through bare telegraph wires overhead while Karski shivered uncontrollably inside his thin overcoat. *"Someone in Warsaw handed the bandits our exact sector boundaries forty-eight hours ahead of H-hour. Worse, that Praga rail timetable you telephoned in last week—Schedule Alpha—was a salted canary trap planted by Oberstleutnant Schoeler's adjutant! Had my clerk forwarded it to Berlin unverified, I should look like an amateur!"*

Karski had practically dropped to his knees in the black cinders when Reinhardt opened his gloved hand to show *only one* five-ampoule strip of morphine instead of twenty—and tapped a red-bordered *Überstellungsbefehl* transfer form bearing Estera Goldblum's name.

*"Vittel camp is overcrowded, Doktor. Non-productive Latin American passport holders without high-value sponsors transfer east to Birkenau on December twenty-eighth. Bring me concrete, prosecutable proof of what passes between Frau Doktor Kotarska and Oberstleutnant Schoeler inside Number Twelve Aleja Róż before Christmas Eve—something written, photographed, or overheard—or File Four-Nineteen-B boards the Silesian train."*

Now, hurrying out of Sylwia's fluoroscopy booth toward the locked doctors' lavatory with that lone five-ampoule morphine strip burning against his ribs, Roman Karski didn't feel guilt anymore—only a blind, hunted rat's certainty that either Sylwia Kotarska went down before Christmas, or Estera died in Birkenau gas.

Five hours later, inside the warm study on Aleja Róż while snow rattled dry against the casements, Ludwig listened silently as Sylwia reported the glowing blue thumbprints under the Wood's lamp.

"Clean and certain," Ludwig said, walking to the map table in riding boots and unbuttoned white shirt—four weeks post-ambush now, color back in his lean cheeks, moving with ninety percent of his lethal pre-war grace. "No risk of hanging an innocent clerk. Leave Karski untouched for four more days."

Sylwia frowned sharply from the hearth chair. "Leave a Gestapo informant walking loose through my surgical wards for four days? If he spots one bandage going down to the boiler room—"

"If Karski drops dead or vanishes tonight," Ludwig cut in crisply, holding up two fingers in classic Generalstab sequence, "two things happen immediately: Reinhardt knows Pavilion Three uncovered *Jaskółka* and raids your hospital before breakfast, AND Reinhardt puts a fresh, unknown shadow onto us. Whereas a known, desperate double-agent whose strings *we* see is worth his weight in gold! Give Viktor until Tuesday to pull Referat Four's actual internal Hotel Polski transport manifest through our clerk at Szucha. Once we hold paper truth on Estera Goldblum, we own Roman Karski's soul—and use him to feed Otto Reinhardt whatever poison we choose."

Logically, surgically, he was unassailable—yet before Tuesday arrived, Warsaw intervened with claws of its own.

***

On the night of December fifteenth, gale-force east winds straight off the Russian steppes drove temperatures down to sixteen below zero and buried ulica Lindleya in waist-high snowdrifts.

At ten-twenty PM, two hours past curfew while Sylwia was finishing septic ward rounds in Pavilion Three, three frantic iron clangs echoed up the empty metal laundry chute from the sub-basement—Halina's emergency distress code.

Snatching her surgical roll without breaking into a visible run down the corridor, Sylwia slipped through the ground-floor sluice pantry, unlocked the narrow iron spiral stair behind the linen presses, and plunged down into the sweltering, steam-thick brick labyrinth behind the hospital's roaring typhus-disinfection boilers.

Behind the false stack of soda-ash barrels where young Janek had lain last month—Janek had been safely moved out to a Milanówek convalescent farm in a potato wagon ten days ago—flickered a single shielded barn lantern.

Kneeling on the brick floor in her blue nurse's cotton dress soaked dark crimson from apron to knees was twenty-six-year-old Halina Nowak, pressing a folded boiled towel desperately over the bare, board-hard, distended abdomen of a gaunt nineteen-year-old boy wearing torn, frost-stiffened *Ostbahn* railway-laborer overalls. Old night porter Władek stood grey-faced by the coal chute bolting the outer iron hatch.

"Halina—" Sylwia dropped straight to her knees beside the straw pallet, peeling back the crimson-soaked towel for two seconds beneath the yellow lantern flame.

One glance told the entire catastrophic story. Two inches left of the umbilicus gaped the small, blackened, powder-singed entry hole of a seven-point-nine-two Mauser carbine bullet; lower toward the right iliac crest bulged a boggy blue-black subcutaneous hematoma where the deformed lead slug lay lodged under the fascia without exiting. Worse—escaping on a thin bubble of gas from the entry track came the unmistakable feculent odor of perforated small intestine, and across both starved, shivering flanks bloomed tiny, mulberry-pink petechial spots that did not blanch under pressure.

Gunshot perforation of the ileum *and* Day Four louse-borne epidemic typhus.

"It's Jacek," Halina whispered. For four years Sylwia had watched Halina Nowak hold retractors steady while Luftwaffe five-hundred-kilo bombs blew window frames inward across the street; tonight Halina's chin quivered like a terrified child's as she looked up at Sylwia. "My little brother, Pani Doktor—they shipped his Praga locomotive gang toward Brest on Monday when typhus broke out in the barracks, he jumped off a slow flatcar at Dworzec Wschodni two hours ago when a *Bahnschutz* guard shot at him in the dark, and crawled three miles across the frozen river ice under Poniatowski Bridge to reach me! Save him—for the love of Christ's Mother, Pani Doktor, my mother died in forty-one, he's all I have left on earth!"

On the pallet, nineteen-year-old Jacek Nowak—Halina's dark eyes, cracked bleeding lips crusted with frostbite, pulse hammering at one hundred thirty-six—clutched Sylwia's white sleeve with burning, lice-bitten fingers: *"Don't let them take me back to the boxcars, Pani—"*

Before Sylwia could even call for sterile water, two sharp knocks rattled the upper sluice pipe, and Professor Tadeusz Majewski came hurrying down the spiral iron steps two at a time, spectacles fogged instantaneously by boiler steam, white beard trembling violently against his unbuttoned collar.

"Sylwia! God help us—Władek, did he drop blood in the snow coming through the Nowogrodzka coal lane?!"

Władek blanched under coal dust. "Wind was drifting heavy, Pan Profesor, I swept the gate step with a broom, maybe ten yards out in the alley—"

"Two *Bahnschutz* railway patrol trucks and a *Schutzpolizei* squad just turned off Chałubińskiego following blood spots in the drift with two Alsatian tracking dogs!" Majewski gasped, gripping the iron handrail to stay upright. "They are at the main Lindleya courtyard gate right now demanding to search Pavilion Three and the cellars for an escaped railway saboteur! We can't move him out—every gate is blocked!"

In the suffocating brick vault beneath the hospital, nobody breathed.

Look upward: five feet directly above Jacek Nowak's straw pallet, set flush into the vaulted brick ceiling to give daytime light to the outer laundry passage on the other side of an inch-thick pine plank partition behind the soda-ash barrels, ran a six-foot square grid of thick green glass pavement-blocks forming the floor of the covered ground-floor courtyard arcade.

If an Alsatian tracking dog smelled fresh human blood down the service stairs behind the laundry vats—or heard one groan through those glass ceiling blocks—every soul in this basement went against the Nowy Świat brick wall tomorrow noon, and Reinhardt wouldn't need Karski anymore.

And if Sylwia waited forty minutes to open Jacek Nowak's peritoneum while liquid feces poured out of torn ileum into a typhus-weakened bloodstream, irreversible endotoxic shock killed Halina's brother before midnight anyway.

Inside Sylwia's brain, that absolute, glacial battlefield stillness snapped cleanly into command.

"Tadeusz!" Sylwia ordered swiftly, tearing off her outer ward coat and plunging bare hands and forearms straight into Władek's bucket of hot sublimate solution beside the boiler tap while Halina tore open the emergency tin drum of boiled laparotomy linen they kept permanently hidden inside Dummy Boiler Two. "Run straight up to Maternity Delivery Ward One—Sister Zofia delivered twin placentas twenty minutes ago, the enamel slop-pail hasn't gone to the incinerator yet! Take Władek, dump both fresh placentas and two liters of retroplacental blood right across the top of the cellar coal steps and the outer laundry passage, and pour half a carboy of raw fifty-percent carbolic cresol and dry chloride of lime straight on top of it!"

Old Professor Majewski's eyes widened behind misted lenses—catching the sheer, cunning brilliance of it in half a second.

"Human blood at the top of the stairs explains any yard scent as careless hospital incinerator slops," Sylwia hissed rapidly, shaking stinging sublimate off her bare elbows as Halina snapped dry patched rubber gloves onto her hands, "and raw cresol plus chlorine gas burns an Alsatian dog's olfactory mucosa blind for two hours if it puts its snout within six feet! Tell the *Bahnschutz* sergeant we burn typhus sheets and septic afterbirths down here and invite him warmly to come sniff typhus lice himself if he doubts you! Move—now!"

Majewski and Władek scrambled up the spiral iron steps faster than men twenty years younger.

Down behind the soda-ash partition—a brick niche seven feet long by five feet wide, eighty-eight degrees Fahrenheit from the adjacent boiler flue—Sylwia dropped onto both knees in dirty brick dust beside Jacek's low wooden trestle-pallet. No zinc table down here; surgeons who couldn't cut on their knees in cellars belonged in Zurich.

"Strip his overalls away from xiphoid to pubis—scissors, don't pull cloth over the wound—and paint everything drowned in iodine," Sylwia whispered, voice pitched lower than the hiss of steam in the copper boiler valve four feet away. "You handle the ether cone, Halina—open drop, fast induction so he doesn't cry out during the excitement stage when they enter the courtyard. If boots step onto those glass bricks overhead before he's deep in Plane Three, clamp a folded wet towel over the wire mask even if he turns blue for twenty seconds. Understand?"

Halina wiped her wet cheeks once on her shoulder—gone was the weeping sister; back stood Pavilion Three's finest scrub nurse, jaw set like Mazovian oak.

"Understood, Pani Doktor."

"Hold my wrist, Jacek," Sylwia murmured softly, touching the boy's burning temple for one second as Halina lowered the Schimmelbusch wire cone over his frost-chapped nose and tipped raw ether fast onto eight layers of gauze. "Count backward from twenty in your head for Halina. In thirty minutes your belly won't hurt."

Jacek swallowed hard beneath the choking sweet-arctic fumes, eyes rolling wildly once at the second breath as delirious typhus muscle fought ether—his boots kicked slightly toward the soda barrels; Sylwia threw one knee smoothly across both his shins without bruising bone, holding him silent as stone while Halina poured ether recklessly close to saturation until his pupils centered, eyeballs fixed, and diaphragmatic breathing settled deep and automatic.

Upstairs—muffled through vaulted brick, then abruptly loud and sharp directly overhead—heavy iron courtyard gates banged wide, and frantic, deep-chested Alsatian barking echoed off stone arcades alongside shouted German commands!

*"Such, Prinz! Fass! Da drüben beim Kohlenschacht!"*

*Search, Prince! Over there by the coal shaft!*

Sylwia did not look up at the frosted green glass blocks five feet above her crown. Holding a #10 scalpel steady in her right hand under the turned-down yellow lantern wick, she drew a clean six-inch left paramedian incision from two inches above the umbilicus straight down through rectus sheath and transversalis fascia, nicked the bulging, purple-black peritoneum carefully between two hemostats—a foul hiss of trapped intestinal gas and dark blood-stained fecal fluid welled up into Halina's silent hand-sponge—and slid four gloved fingers gently down into the blazing heat of the boy's abdominal cavity.

Directly above her bent dark head, hobnailed boots stepped squarely onto the thick green glass pavement-blocks of the arcade floor—black boot-soles distorted like giant beetles four feet above the lantern, accompanied by the scrabbling iron claws of a ninety-pound Alsatian straining on a leather choke-chain!

Halina's hand froze over the ether bottle, eyes locked in terror on the black boot-shapes overhead; the lantern flame was shielded underneath a tin stove-hood so zero yellow light leaked upward through frosted glass coated in six inches of packed snow, yet six feet of air and mortared glass was all that stood between two women kneeling in human blood and six Mauser carbines.

From twenty yards away at the top of the outer laundry steps drifted Professor Majewski's indignant, aristocratic Vienna German, ringing out magnificently across the echoing arcade:

*"Aber meine Herren, ich warne Sie ausdrücklich—hier unten liegt die Fleckfieber-Wäscherei und der Verbrennungsschacht für septische Nachgeburten! Władek hat gerade Chlorkalk über einen umgekippten Eimer geschüttet—wenn Ihre Hunde Läuse aus den Fleckfieberdecken fangen, bringen Sie das Fleckfieber morgen früh direkt in Ihre eigene Kaserne!"*

*Fleckfieber!*

No single German word east of the Oder cleared space faster than *typhus*. Every soldier from Brest to Kharkov lived in mortal dread of body lice carrying Rickettsia.

Ten yards down the steps came a sudden, explosive canine yelp followed by furious sneezing and hacking coughs as two Alsatians plunged eager muzzles straight into two liters of maternity blood drenched in raw fifty-percent cresol and blistering chlorine gas!

*"Verdammte Scheiße, Feldwebel, der Hund kotzt sich die Lunge raus—alles voll Nachgeburt und Karbolgestank da unten, und Fleckfieber-Wäsche dampft im Kessel!"* coughed a handler's disgusted voice, scrambling backward up onto the glass blocks directly above Sylwia's shoulders.

Down in the eighty-eight-degree brick niche, knees aching on gritty stone, Sylwia hadn't paused for half a second.

Exteriorizing a bruised, crimson-purple loop of mid-ileum onto wet warm saline towels across Jacek's belly, she found the culprit immediately: the tumbling Mauser bullet had blown two ragged holes three centimeters apart right across the mesenteric border of the ileum, Thrombosing a wedge of mesenteric arcade and turning four inches of small bowel plum-black and dead, before lodging harmlessly inside the right internal oblique muscle wall.

No simple patch would hold; leave dead gut inside him and gangrene perforated tomorrow.

"Four non-crushing Doyen intestinal clamps—milk the bowel contents back six inches each way with your fingers, Halina, don't click steel ratchets loud," Sylwia breathed without moving her lips, communicating half by hand-sign in the amber gloom while boots still scraped undecidedly across the glass bricks four feet overhead.

Pinching the bowel lumen gently shut between Halina's steady fingers instead of ratcheting noisy metal teeth, Sylwia divided the necrotic four-inch segment cleanly with curved scissors, ligated three bleeding mesenteric vessels with fine silk, and laid the two healthy pink bowel ends squarely face-to-face for a two-layer hand-sewn end-to-end anastomosis.

Every stitch had to be watertight to the fraction of a millimeter: inner continuous locking Connell suture through all coats to invert mucosa cleanly without narrowing the lumen; outer ring of twenty-four interrupted fine silk Lembert seromuscular stitches three millimeters apart—*in, graze submucosa, out, square throw, cut*—her fingers flying in a silent, hypnotic blur learned across ten years and three thousand hours of trauma while sweat ran down between her shoulder blades under her smock and dripped off the tip of her chin onto sterile green toweling.

Overhead, the *Bahnschutz* sergeant spat loudly, barked *"Weiter zur Nowogrodzka—durch den Dreck kriecht kein Angeschossener ohne tot umzufallen!"* and marched his sneezing dogs and cursing patrol back out through the courtyard gates into the howling blizzard.

Neither woman breathed out until the distant truck engines ground into first gear and faded down Chałubińskiego.

Even then Sylwia didn't slow: scooping the deformed lead Mauser slug swiftly out of the right abdominal wall pocket with two fingers, lavaging the peritoneal cavity four times with warm boiled saline, dusting her last treasured five grams of German *Marfanil* sulfonamide powder straight over the sutured bowel ring before closing peritoneum and fascia with heavy figure-of-eight catgut and leaving the contaminated skin open around an iodoform wick.

Nineteen minutes from skin knife to final dressing.

Sliding two precious stolen ampoules of Słowik's *Cibazol* into Jacek's arm vein to fight peritonitis while typhus ran its twelve-day fever furnace, Sylwia stripped off her soaked rubber gloves and sank back onto her heels against a sack of soda ash, pushing a sweat-drenched strand of hair off her forehead with the back of her forearm.

Across the sleeping boy's blanketed knees, Halina Nowak caught Sylwia's bare, sublimate-reddened right hand without warning, bent her forehead straight down onto Sylwia's knuckles, and kissed them once—fiercely, wordlessly—before wiping her face dry and standing up to check the pulse.

"Don't thank me before Day Eight when typhus hits his myocardium, Halina," Sylwia murmured wearily, climbing to her stiff knees. "Nobody comes down this stair except you, Władek, and me—least of all Roman Karski."

Halina's dark eyes snapped sharp instantly at the tone around *Roman Karski*. She didn't ask why—in *Konspiracja* nurses didn't ask questions that cost lives—she simply nodded once like an axe falling: "If Karski touches that sluice latch, Pani Doktor, Władek puts a coal shovel through his ear."
