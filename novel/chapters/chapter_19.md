# CHAPTER NINETEEN

## The Executioner's Ledger

**Warsaw — February 1, 1944**

After six weeks of killer Russian frost, the morning of Tuesday, February first dawned in eerie, dripping thaw.

Dirty grey fog rolled off the half-frozen Vistula at one degree above zero, cloaking bare lime trees along Aleje Ujazdowskie in ghostly silver droplets and turning blackened snowbanks beside the tram rails into treacherous slush.

Parked nose-out sixty yards down quiet ulica Litewska with its motor idling softly in white exhaust vapor sat a mud-splashed field-grey Wehrmacht *Sanitäts-Kraftwagen* Opel Blitz ambulance—requisitioned permanently at midnight from a sleeping motor-pool corporal in Praga with Słowik's chloroform rag. Up on the high bench beside a steel-nerved twenty-two-year-old Kedyw driver in Wehrmacht corporal's wool sat Oberstleutnant Ludwig Schoeler.

Pressed field-grey greatcoat buttoned immaculate to the throat, crimson General Staff collar tabs gleaming damply through mist, silver bullion peaked cap tilted one fraction lower over his brow, Ludwig rested both leather-gloved hands atop a captured Bergmann submachine gun hidden beneath a folded stretcher blanket across his knees. On his hip rode his loaded nine-millimeter Luger; inside his left breast pocket, directly over the solid bone-callus of his healed clavicle, rode two ounces of dented Berlin silver.

Directly behind his shoulder on the ambulance's wooden medic bench sat Sylwia.

Clad in the slate-grey wool cape, starched white linen coif, and red-cross enamel brooch of a Reich-German *Deutsches Rotes Kreuz* surgical sister—face pale and aristocratic enough beneath white linen that no German sentry ever looked twice—she held her open black leather doctor's bag wedged steady between her boots. Beneath six bottles of sterile dextran plasma, morphine ampoules, and bolt-cutters lay a cocked Walther PPK and a six-inch straight Liston amputating knife honed until it split falling hair.

 Ludwig glanced once at his wristwatch: **09:07**.

Out behind the snow-covered brick garden wall of Number Eight Litewska fifty meters ahead, invisible among dead privet hedges, Marek Zawadzki and three hand-picked Kedyw veterans waited flat in slush with wire-shears poised against inner chain-link. Inside that quiet three-story neoclassical villa, behind barred basement coal-grilles, ticked the final two hours and fifty-three minutes before Otto Reinhardt boarded Junkers Ju-52 courier flight *Ost-Vier* at Okęcie airfield with Viktor Brandt in leg-irons and forty-two Polish death warrants locked inside black calfskin.

Through the small sliding glass window between cab and ambulance box, Ludwig turned his head slightly to meet Sylwia's storm-grey eyes under her white Red Cross veil.

Neither wasted luck wishing safety.

"Four minutes inside the house maximum," Ludwig murmured in quiet German. "If we aren't climbing back into this tailboard by nine-sixteen, second-wave SS armor seals Ujazdów Park and Poniatowski Bridge together. Stay three paces behind my right shoulder until the cellar stairs open."

"Watch your left flank on stairs," Sylwia answered evenly, touching two gloved fingers once against the back of his wrist before pulling the cab window shut. "My collarbone work isn't warrantied against point-blank Schmeissers."

**09:08.**

Up on broad Aleje Ujazdowskie two hundred eighty meters north—completely invisible behind mist-shrouded stone corners where SS-Brigadeführer Franz Kutschera's grey Adler limousine had just chauffeured the butcher of Warsaw out of his mansion on Aleja Róż toward SS Headquarters at the Palais Leszczyński—a twenty-year-old Polish boy named *Lot* swung an old Adler sedan squarely across the wet tramway to block Kutschera's bumper.

At **09:09 and twenty seconds**, all hell tore the damp morning mist apart.

First came the unmistakable, ear-splitting chatter of two British Sten submachine guns and a German MP-40 firing full thirty-two-round bursts in echoing stone canyon—*BRRRRRRP! TAK-TAK-TAK-TAK!*—followed three seconds later by the heavy, window-rattling *CRUMP!* of a Filipinka grenade and frantic German whistle-blasts, pistol cracks, and tires screaming across slick cobbles!

Down on quiet ulica Litewska, flocks of wet crows exploded black out of bare chestnut crowns.

Instantly the front iron gate of Number Eight Litewska burst inward from the courtyard side as seven helmeted SD *Wachmänner* tumbled down the villa porch steps unslinging rifles and staring wildly northward toward the billowing grey smoke-plume rising over Ujazdowskie. *"Anschlag beim Brigadeführer! Zur Ujazdowskie—schnell!"* roared a sergeant, waving five of his seven guards sprinting at the double up toward the corner intersection to reinforce SS Headquarters while leaving only two nervous young troopers holding the iron driveway grille!

"Roll!" Ludwig barked at the Kedyw driver.

Gears meshed; the heavy grey Wehrmacht ambulance surged sixty yards forward, horn blaring twice imperiously, and braked hard nose-to-grille directly against the iron gates of Number Eight Litewska while distant pistol fire still popped fiercely three blocks north!

Before the ambulance tires stopped rocking on wet slush, Ludwig kicked open the passenger door and strode straight to the iron bars—six feet two of towering Prussian General Staff fury, crimson lapels blazing, thrusting Słowik's crimson-bordered RSHA *Sofortbefehl* inches from the startled nineteen-year-old Austrian SD sentry's nose.

*"Tor auf, Mann, oder haben Sie Dreck in den Ohren?!"* Ludwig thundered in blistering Potsdam parade-ground *Kommandosprache* that made both young guards snap spines rigid before their brains caught up. *"Großangriff polnischer Banditen auf das SS-Hauptquartier Ujazdowskie—Brigadeführer Kutschera getroffen! Auf Befehl Aleja Szucha werden sämtliche Geheimakten und Häftlinge aus Keller Acht sofort unter Wehrmachtsschutz nach Okęcie ausgeflogen, bevor der zweite Banditenzug hier durchbricht! Aufschließen—sofort!"*

*Terrorist attack on SS HQ—Kutschera hit! Immediate evacuation of cellar files and prisoners to Okęcie before the second bandit wave hits here!*

With gunfire still crackling audibly up on Ujazdowskie and sirens beginning to wail madly across three districts at once, neither rattled sentry dreamed of questioning a crimson-striped Wehrmacht colonel screaming RSHA emergency codes. Iron bolts clanged back; both heavy gates swung wide; the Opel ambulance roared straight inside the gravel courtyard and spun one hundred eighty degrees on locked brakes so its rear double doors backed six feet from the villa's marble entrance steps!

Simultaneously, out behind the snowy carriage-house lilacs at the rear of the yard, two muffled *phut-clack* snaps cut garden chain-link as Marek Zawadzki and three white-trenchcoated Kedyw gunmen glided silent as wolves up to the cellar coal-windows!

Four seconds more and they would have owned the ground floor without firing a round.

Then the tall oak front doors of the villa swung open above the marble porch, and burly *Kriminalsekretär* Krauss—who had interrogated Tomek down in Cell Four and smashed Viktor Brandt's cheekbone at Dworzec Główny nine nights ago—stepped out onto the top granite step carrying a cocked MP-40 across his chest.

Twelve yards separated them. Fog drifted thin between porch pillars.

Krauss blinked once past the Red Cross nurse climbing out the rear ambulance step, locked straight onto the lean, hawk-boned face beneath the silver-peaked General Staff cap, spotted the white crescent scar above the right eyebrow—and bellowed at the top of his lungs:

*"ALARM! DAS IST SCHOELER—SCHIESSEN!"*

Krauss never got his Schmeisser muzzle down two inches.

Ludwig dropped his paper clipboard cleanly into the slush, swept the cocked Bergmann MP-35 up from beneath his unbuttoned greatcoat skirt in one fluid blur learned across four years of front-line war, and squeezed two tight three-round bursts from the hip—*brrrp! brrrp!*

The first burst stitched left-to-right across the right gate sentry's chest as the boy fumbled wildly at his Mauser sling, slamming him flat onto his back into dirty snow; the second caught *Kriminalsekretär* Krauss squarely across stomach and right collarbone at ten paces, spinning two hundred ten pounds of Gestapo butcher sideways through the porch balustrade in a spray of splintered stone and bright arterial red!

Left gate sentry dove frantically behind the brick gate-pillar raising his rifle straight at Ludwig's exposed flank—and pitched forward lifeless onto his face as Marek Zawadzki's suppressed Sten coughed *chug-chug-chug* from behind the lilac corner twenty yards right!

"Clear the hall—cellar stairs rear left!" Marek shouted in Polish, vaulting the broken balustrade over groaning, gut-shot Krauss alongside two Kedyw boys while the third stayed guarding the running ambulance motor at the gate!

Dying on the wet marble steps with crimson froth bubbling over his lips, Krauss made one final convulsive lunge backward with his blood-slick left fist and yanked down a red-painted iron fire-lever recessed beside the vestibule wainscot.

Down beneath the house, an electric klaxon buzzed twice and heavy electromagnetic latches slammed a two-inch steel bulkhead door shut across the bottom of the basement stairs!

"Plastic on the lower hinges—ten seconds, stand back!" barked Marek's sapper *Wilk*, plunging down the narrow whitewashed cellar steps ahead of Ludwig, Marek, and Sylwia to slap two grey sausages of British Nobel 808 plastic explosive molded around a five-second crimper fuse directly against the steel doorframe.

Five steps up around the stone bend, Ludwig pulled Sylwia hard back against his chest behind six inches of brick corner pier, shielding her face into his field-grey wool just as the charge blew.

*WHUMP-CRACK!*

Hot yellow cordite flash and pulverized lime dust roared up the stairwell like cannon breath, tearing the steel door twisted and smoking clean off both lower hinges into the cellar passage below!

Ears ringing deafeningly inside acrid grey smoke, Marek and Ludwig went through the breached frame low and fast together—Marek swinging left, Ludwig right—straight into Referat Four's brightly lit concrete interrogation cellar.

Two black-uniformed SS *Scharführer* crouched behind an overturned oak filing table thirty feet down the whitewashed vault firing furiously into the smoke!

Ludwig's Bergmann roared flat from behind a brick furnace pier—taking the right-hand SS guard clean through throat and forehead over the table rim—while Marek triggered a long Sten burst that stitched the second guard sideways into steel lockers, half a heartbeat before the dying German's falling MP-40 sprayed four wild nine-millimeter rounds upward across the passage!

Two rounds sparked harmlessly off brick; two punched loud wet *thwack-thwack* holes straight through the front meat of Marek Zawadzki's left deltoid and fractured acromion, spinning Marek violently backward against the tiled wall sliding down onto one knee cursing fiercely through clenched teeth in Polish as dark blood poured down his white trenchcoat sleeve!

"Stay down on the wall, Grot—artery's intact, pack your glove under the armpit!" Sylwia snapped, dropping two seconds beside Marek to jam a sterile field pad tight into his bleeding shoulder crease before lifting her head through thinning grey cordite smoke toward the rear steel vault arch.

And froze.

Chained thirty inches apart along the back concrete wall under glaring bare bulbs hung two battered human beings.

On the left, suspended half-standing with both bruised, swollen wrists padlocked high around an overhead cast-iron ceiling pipe so his boot-toes barely scraped wet concrete, hung Hauptmann Viktor Brandt—uniform shirt ripped to bloody ribbons across purpled ribs, left eye beaten completely shut into black-violet pulp, three fingers of his right hand splinted crudely crooked where pliers had broken phalanges, single open blue right eye blazing unbowed sanity as he spotted his colonel through the smoke.

Six feet right, handcuffed low by her right wrist to the heavy steam radiator while her broken left collarbone hung unsupported and purple beneath a torn blue cotton nurse's dress, knelt **Halina Nowak**—split lip crusted dark, dark hair matted with dried blood—because standing squarely behind Halina inside the open door of the rear emergency coal-tunnel leading toward Aleja Szucha's back garages stood **SS-Sturmbannführer Otto Reinhardt**.

Blast concussion had cracked the left lens of Reinhardt's gold-wired spectacles cleanly in two across his pale freckled cheek, where a thin red line of plaster-cut blood trickled neatly into his mouse-grey collar. Tucked securely beneath his left elbow gleamed the thick, brass-clasped black calfskin spine of the **Crown Ledger**; pressed dead flat against the bare pulsing skin behind Halina Nowak's right ear rested the blued muzzle of Reinhardt's cocked Walther PPK.

Twenty feet away across blood-splashed concrete, Ludwig froze with his smoking Bergmann leveled chest-high—knowing instantly that at twenty feet in smoky light, even if nine millimeters took Reinhardt between the spectacles, reflex finger-spasm blew Halina Nowak's brainstem out across the radiator inside the same tenth of a second.

Out in the distant streets north toward Ujazdowskie, twenty converging German police sirens howled into an unbroken metallic shriek. Three minutes left at best.

"Set the submachine gun gently onto the floor tiles, Herr Oberstleutnant," Otto Reinhardt said. Even with half a cracked lens and gunpowder soot dusting his neat hair, his quiet Munich detective's voice didn't rise half a tone above conversational parlor pitch. "And Captain *Grot* by the wall—keep your right hand well clear of that Sten, please. One millimeter's twitch and Frau Doktor Kotarska loses her favorite nurse before noon after all."

"Don't drop it, Pani Doktor—shoot the bastard through me!" Halina croaked fiercely in Polish, trying to wrench her bruised throat sideways until Reinhardt twisted a fistful of her dark hair backward so tightly tears sprang blind into her eyes.

Ludwig's pale slate eyes didn't look at Halina; they stayed locked straight onto Reinhardt's spectacles—except for one microscopic quarter-second when Ludwig shifted his left boot half an inch rightward, the exact stance he used to take at the Reichstagsufer chalkboard before throwing a blind pass across to Sylwia's bench.

Standing three paces right of Ludwig in her grey German Red Cross cape and white veil, right hand resting casually inside the open mouth of her black surgical bag at her hip, Sylwia read that half-inch shift of his boot heel across sixteen years as clearly as shouted print.

*He drops steel left to pull Reinhardt's eyes three degrees off me—and Reinhardt won't shoot a hostage first if he thinks he has a clean shot at Ludwig's heart.*

"Your Berlin plane leaves without you at twelve, Reinhardt," Ludwig said coldly, bending one knee slowly to lay the heavy Bergmann submachine gun clattering down onto wet concrete—deliberately letting it slide two feet *leftward* toward Marek's boot so Reinhardt's cracked lenses tracked the sliding steel instinctively for half a second. "Canaris or no Canaris, Kutschera is lying dead three blocks north right now on *your* security watch, and fifty Wehrmacht witnesses upstairs just heard your house blow up."

"Kaltenbrunner forgives broken windows when one brings him Tresckow's Warsaw courier and the *Kronenbuch* in the same valise," Reinhardt smiled faintly—and just as Ludwig had calculated to the fraction of an inch, having watched the submachine gun leave Ludwig's hands, greedy Munich perfectionism couldn't resist taking the tall Prussian aristocrat alive for meat hooks rather than wasting his penultimate bullet on a Polish nurse: Reinhardt swung the Walther muzzle four inches up off Halina's ear straight toward Ludwig's center-mass—no, thigh—to drop Schoeler crippled on the floor before backing down the coal-tunnel!

*Crack!*

Even as Ludwig pivoted sideways lunging forward at twenty feet, the 7.65-millimeter jacket punched red-hot straight through the outer muscle of Ludwig's left thigh four inches above the knee—knocking his left leg out from under him onto wet concrete while his right hand clawed toward his hip holster!

Reinhardt swung the blued barrel two inches higher to put the second round cleanly through Ludwig's forehead—

And twenty-six-year-old Halina Nowak, freed for one glorious heartbeat from the muzzle at her ear, drove the back of her skull upward with every ounce of Wola fury left in her spine straight into the bridge of Otto Reinhardt's nose!

Gold-wired spectacles shattered across his face in a blinding spray of nasal blood!

Reinhardt stumbled half a step back against the open vault doorframe firing blind into ceiling plaster—*pop!*—just as Sylwia Kotarska crossed fifteen feet of cordite smoke without making a sound.

She didn't draw the Walther inside her bag because bullets miss in smoke and steel never misses when a surgeon holds the handle.

Catching Reinhardt's gun-wrist outward with her left hand before he could bring the pistol down toward Ludwig's chest, slamming his narrow mouse-grey shoulders flat back against the steel vault jamb, Sylwia raised six inches of razor-honed Liston amputating steel in her steady right fist, found the soft hollow of the suprasternal notch directly above his manubrium sterni between both collarbones without even needing to look down, and drove four and a half inches of surgical Sheffield steel downward at thirty degrees straight into his superior mediastinum—severing brachiocephalic trunk, aortic arch branch, and anterior tracheal wall in one single, clean, unstoppable thrust.

Warm scarlet blood burst up around the brass guard over Sylwia's bare wrist and Red Cross grey cape.

For four endless, silent seconds beneath the glaring two-hundred-watt cellar bulb, Otto Reinhardt stood pinned upright against the iron vault frame six inches from Sylwia's pale face—Walther dropping *clink* out of nerveless fingers onto concrete, mouth opening twice without a whisper of air able to climb past severed windpipe, pale freckled eyes staring wide and uncomprehending minus spectacles straight into Sylwia Kotarska's winter-storm grey gaze as Zbigniew Milewski, nineteen-year-old Tomek, gentle old Tadeusz Majewski, Estera Goldblum, and forty thousand nameless ghosts looked coldly back at him behind her pupils.

Then Sylwia wrenched the blade cleanly free, stepped sideways out of the splash, and let Referat Four's chief slide crumpled onto his side into the coal dust, heels twitching once before going forever still.

Nobody cheered. Professionals in hell didn't waste seconds gloating.

Dragging himself upright onto his bleeding left leg—clean muscle through-and-through lateral to the femur, missing femoral artery by two inches—Ludwig snatched up his Luger, blew both brass padlocks off Viktor Brandt's overhead wrist-chains and Halina's radiator cuff in two deafening nine-millimeter shots after wrapping thick wool blankets over their hands to catch brass splinters, and bent beside Reinhardt's motionless corpse to pry the blood-spattered, brass-clasped black leather **Crown Ledger** out from beneath the dead detective's slack arm.

"Can you walk, Viktor?!" Ludwig barked, throwing Brandt's intact left arm squarely across his own broad shoulders despite both men limping red across the tiles.

"Walk to Königsberg before I stay another night in this pigsty, Herr Oberstleutnant," croaked scarred Viktor Brandt through swollen purple lips, spitting blood and grinning fiercely out of his single open eye.

Sylwia had already slung her grey cape over Halina's broken left collarbone, binding Halina's arm immobile against her chest in ten seconds with a triangular bandage while Marek's sapper *Wilk* braced one-handed Marek up the blasted cellar stairs!

Nine-fifteen AM sharp—five minutes and forty seconds from front gate to courtyard gravel!

Out on Litewska and three blocks north along Ujazdowskie, total, glorious pandemonium reigned just as predicted: German police trucks, screaming motorcycles, and Wehrmacht staff cars jammed bumper-to-bumper around Kutschera's bullet-riddled grey Adler sedan while rumors of parachute landings and full Polish uprising sent sirens roaring wildly north toward Krakowskie Przedmieście.

When a mud-spattered Wehrmacht *Sanitäts-Kraftwagen* ambulance bearing a crimson-tabbed General Staff colonel slumped pale in the passenger seat roared east out of Litewska waving frantic red emergency flags toward Poniatowski Bridge and the Praga military hospital sidings, two frantic *Schutzpolizei* traffic constables actually blew whistles and waved civilian carts aside to clear their path across the Vistula!

Inside the swaying canvas-covered ambulance box rattling sixty kilometers an hour high above dark, ice-choked Vistula water on Poniatowski Bridge, Sylwia knelt between stretchers packing sulfonamide gauze tight into Ludwig's bleeding left quadriceps with one hand while checking Marek's shattered left shoulder dressing and Halina's splinted collarbone with the other.

Propped pale against the front partition beside half-conscious Viktor Brandt, Ludwig reached down with blood-streaked fingers, lifted Otto Reinhardt's heavy black leather **Crown Ledger**, and held it out across the narrow aisle toward Marek Zawadzki.

"Thirty-eight active Gestapo informants and forty-two *Wintersturm* arrest addresses inside, Captain," Ludwig said quietly over the roar of bridge girders beneath the tires. "Burn the cover once you copy the names."

One-armed, white-lipped with bone pain from his shattered left shoulder, Marek took the blood-stained black ledger into his good right hand, stared down at the silver SS runes embossed on calfskin for two silent heartbeats, and then lifted his dark, battle-hardened eyes across the swaying ambulance straight into Ludwig Schoeler's.

Remembering his threat in the Leszno bunker thirty hours ago—*once that ledger reaches us, your usefulness expires*—Marek glanced once at Sylwia's pale profile bent over Ludwig's bleeding thigh, then back into the Prussian's unflinching slate gaze.

Slowly, Marek shifted the ledger under his bleeding arm and extended his bare, powder-stained right hand across the aisle.

"Safe journey tonight from Dworzec Wschodni, Colonel," Marek Zawadzki said quietly in rough schoolboy German. "Don't wear field-grey back across the Vistula before peace comes; next time I might not miss."

Ludwig gripped the Polish engineer's hand once—firm, dry, clean between men who owed zero apologies and zero quarter—and nodded. "See that you keep Warsaw standing until we finish Berlin, Captain."
