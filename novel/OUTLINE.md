# RANA — 20-Chapter Outline

**Logline:** An enemy surgeon in occupied Warsaw holds the life of the one man she ever truly knew how to hate — and the Home Army wants him dead.

**Three-act shape:** Part I — The Enemy on the Table (Ch 1–5) · Part II — The Convalescence (Ch 6–12) · Part III — The City Burns (Ch 13–20)

**POV:** Close third person. Primarily Sylwia; Ludwig takes the lens in Ch 4 and Ch 18.

---

## Part I — The Enemy on the Table

**1. The Officer on the Table** — Warsaw, November 1943. A night of snow and curfew. A Wehrmacht convoy arrives without lights, bearing a critically wounded Oberleutnant. Against the ether and the blood, Sylwia recognizes Ludwig Schoeler. The order from the officer in charge: he lives. Her hands are already scrubbing. Ends inside the cut of the operation — no resolution yet.

**2. Two of Berlin** — Flashback, 1928. Berlin. Friedrich-Wilhelms-Gymnasium, Schöneberg. Sylwia, daughter of a Polish railway engineer posted to the German capital, first sees Ludwig Schoeler — fencing bronze at his hip, his father's Iron Cross in his eyes — and despises him perfectly. Ends with the first open collision (a matter of a stolen theorem, a slapped wrist, an absolutely public humiliation).

**3. The Feud** — 1929–30. The legend of the Kotarska–Schoeler feud: mathematics olympiad; the fencing bout; the rowing regatta on the Havel; the valedictory debate; faculty attempts to separate them failing; the Berlin press of schoolboys ringing the duel. Beneath the hate: the two most alert minds in the school, addicted to each other. Ends with a teacher's warning — "you will spend your lives either destroying each other or finishing each other's sentences."

**4. What the War Made of Them** — Ludwig POV. 1931–36. National socialism rises through the gymnasium corridors; Ludwig, groomed for artillery, absorbs the new air; Sylwia stands at the barricades of her own pride and her own conscience. His mother dead, his father hardening; her father recalled to Warsaw; the last day of school, 1932 — a dropped glove, an unfinished sentence neither finishes. He watches her go. He never writes.

**5. Warsaw, Occupied** — Return to 1943. The war as Sylwia has lived it: siege of '39, her father's death in the hospital's cellar, the roundups, the Jewish wards emptied by SS trucks in '42–43, her brother Tadek vanished into the Home Army (Armia Krajowa), the hospital's German patients, and her own double life — surgeon by day, keeper of secrets by night. Ends where Ch 1 left off: the dressing is off Ludwig's chest, and the bullet he took was meant for something heavier than his heart.

## Part II — The Convalescence

**6. Ward of Enemies** — Ludwig wakes. Guards outside. The old feud rekindled as a duel of wills across a hospital bed: she controls his morphine, he controls his mouth. First fragments of who he has become. Introduction of SS-Kriminalrat Brenner, who comes to inspect "his officer" — and to count the beds in the Jewish wing.

**7. Terms of Engagement** — Ludwig, feverish, tells her fragments: his unit was escorting a convoy when partisan fire hit him; his orderlies fled or were killed; the Wehrmacht believes it was a sniper, and Brenner believes it was the hospital. Sylwia extracts the truth in installments. He was carrying, or knew, the contents of a dossier — Brenner's file.

**8. Blood and Fire** — Clinical crisis: empyema, fever of 40.3, an open-chest drainage that saves him. In the night watch, the feud gutters into something raw and unnameable. She holds his hand through the dark and hates herself for it. Almost-words cross the bed.

**9. Brenner Comes Calling** — Daytime inspection. Hidden Jewish child in the linen store, smuggled morphine in the boots of a dead partisan, an AK courier posing as a porter. Ludwig, semi-conscious, chooses — with his eyes, deliberately — not to hear what she needs him not to hear. Brenner leaves with a date and a warning: one more irregularity, and the hospital burns like Wolska Street will.

**10. The Old Berlin Between Them** — Flashback, summer 1930. The Grünewald lake, the summer boat, the race Sylwia wins by a canvas and Ludwig never forgives — and cannot. The night of the gymnasium's summer fest: dancing, rain, and a kiss in a doorway that neither of them survived intact. How two people came to love each other with their fists clenched.

**11. The Ledger** — Present. Sylwia demands he choose: surrender the dossier to her, or the Home Army chooses for him. Ludwig, gravely weakened but unbroken, offers the terrible bargain — his life for the file's contents, for which Warsaw has waited three years: names, dates, the plan for the city's final solution of the Polish intelligentsia and the hospitals when the retreat order comes. The AK high command orders the patient liquidated before the retreat. Sylwia says no — she will not murder a convalescent, not even him, not even now. The oath holds. Barely.

**12. Winter's End** — March 1944. The house of cards holds a while longer: Ludwig walks the corridors with a cane and a death sentence over his convalescence; Tadek surfaces — thinner, armed, hardened, ashamed of how good his sister has become at hiding— and confronts Sylwia: finish him. The night before the decision, she goes to Ludwig's room to kill the feeling, not the patient. Charged, adult, intimate — and stopped at the threshold of explicitness by the bombs of the neighbouring district and by everything they are not permitted to be. She leaves with the decision made, and does not tell him what it is.

## Part III — The City Burns

**13. The Uprising** — 1 August 1944. The city rises at 17:00. The hospital, surrounded, becomes an island of the wounded — AK boys, Wehrmacht deserters by necessity, civilians, collaborators, children. Ludwig, barred from leaving, fights his own fever and his own side's artillery. Tadeusz is hit holding the barricade.

**14. The Terms of Mercy** — Sylwia treats German wounded under the AK's guns while Wehrmacht officers demand the building's surrender; Ludwig bargains with his own uniform across the barricade line — burning his last shred of standing with the Wehrmacht to buy the hospital hours, not days. Brenner, in charge of the comb-out of the district, promises him a rope for it.

**15. Fire on Wolska Street** — Early August 1944. Reinefarth's men sweep Wola and Ochota; hospitals burn with their patients. Threat comes to St. Lazarus's door: evacuate the Poles, deliver the German officer — or the wards go up. Ludwig slips out in disguise to raise his former guns / former friends against Brenner — and is taken.

**16. Pawiak** — September 1944. Ludwig in the cellars of Pawiak awaiting liquidation; Sylwia's choice: betray her wounded to free him? No — trade: the dossier for the man, through the underground's one channel to Brenner. The German collapse in the west makes Brenner nervous — he will deal. The price is monstrous but he pays it. Tadek's fate resolves in the cellars (he lives — or doesn't; decided in the writing of the chapter, and carried forward).

**17. The Fall of Warsaw** — Uprising's end, October 1944. The capitulation; the long march of civilians out of the dead city under German guard. Sylwia and the surviving wards streaming west along with them — and Brenner's jeep always behind. Night of the train at Pruszków: the file changes hands; the betrayal; the firefight in the rail yard; who dies there, decided in the writing.

**18. The Ice and the Embers** — Ludwig POV. 1945. The collapse of the Reich; Russian guns; a prisoner of war; the winter road; Buchenwald's forgotten overflow / a camp hospital where Ludwig, broken, is found by a Russian patrol speaking terrible Polish and immaculate German. He asks for one thing.

**19. The Ledger Balanced** — 1946–48. Warsaw, rubble and reconstruction. Testimony at the trial of the remnants of Brenner's apparatus; the dossier authenticated; a city counting its dead. Sylwia, building a trauma ward in the ruins. A knock — a man with a cane and a general's careful posture and no country. What remains between them, chosen freely at last, in the ruins, is not nothing.

**20. The Oath** — Epilogue, 1951 (or a winter day in the rebuilt capital). Years on: the surgical amphitheatre, her students, his scar; what they made of the lives the war left them; what "never again" is made of, suture by suture; the last image echoing the yarrow, the wound, and the oath.

## Continuity sheet

- **Sylwia Kotarska** — b. 1908, Galicia; Polish Catholic; daughter of Adam Kotarski (railway engineer, died in the 1939 siege); surgeon, Szpital św. Łazarza (fictional, in Ochota district); one of few women surgeons in Poland; speaks immaculate German, French, and a Warsaw patina of prewar Berlin.
- **Ludwig Schoeler** — b. 1907, Berlin; son of Eberhardt Schoeler (artillery general, d. 1941) and Johanna (d. 1933); Wehrmacht Oberleutnant (artillery liaison); wound: through-and-through left chest, lower lobe contusion, missed the heart by a finger's breadth.
- **Supporting:** Tadeusz "Tadek" Kotarski (brother, ~29, Home Army); Prof. Stanisław Kamler (hospital director); Sister Urszula (ward sister); Dr. Bronia Lis (anesthesiologist); Marcin Walski (AK officer, liaison); SS-Kriminalrat Brenner (antagonist); Frau Ott (hospital linen-room keeper, sheltering hidden child); Captain Hesse (Wehrmacht, convoy commander, Ch 1); General der Artillerie Eberhardt Schoeler (dead).
- **Recurring motifs:** yarrow (krwawnik — the wound-herb); the unfinished sentence; ledgers/accounts ("the ledger"); cold and heat; hands.
- **The dossier:** Brenner's file — two years of transport orders, requisition lists, execution ledgers, and the operational order for the liquidation of Warsaw's hospitals and intelligentsia on the retreat ("Aktion Wirbelwind"). Real value for postwar justice and for the city's warning.
